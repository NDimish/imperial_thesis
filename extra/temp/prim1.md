## Subjective

Patient presents with a 3-day history of diarrhea, describing loose, watery stools occurring approximately six to seven times per day, associated with intermittent crampy pain in the left lower abdomen that does not radiate to the back. He reports feeling weak and shaky, and noticed a subjective temperature (felt hot) around 3-4 days ago at symptom onset, though he did not measure his temperature and has not had fever since. He denies sweating or night sweats. He experienced vomiting at the start of symptoms (normal-colored, no blood) but this has since resolved. He reports loss of appetite, tolerating only light foods such as soups and smoothies, and has been able to hold down fluids. No blood noted in stool. He ate takeaway Chinese food with friends about 4 days prior to symptom onset; otherwise no dietary changes. One of his two children has been vomiting but without diarrhea; no one else in the household has similar symptoms. Symptoms have been affecting his daily activities, requiring him to stay close to the toilet; he has continued to go to work despite this, which has been difficult.

- Past medical history: Asthma, well controlled with inhaler use
- No other bowel or gastrointestinal problems
- Medications: Inhaler for asthma; no other medications
- Social history: Non-smoker; does not drink alcohol; works as an accountant; lives with wife and two children

## Objective

## Assessment

Gastroenteritis, likely secondary to recent Chinese takeaway food consumption 4 days prior; possible viral or bacterial etiology.

## Plan

- Conservative management; antibiotics not indicated at this stage
- Encourage oral hydration with fluids
- Recommend oral rehydration solution (e.g., Dioralyte) from pharmacy to replenish electrolytes
- Paracetamol 2 tablets up to four times a day for fever/weakness over the first few days
- Advise taking 2-3 days off work to rest while infection clears
- Follow-up in 3-4 days if symptoms have not improved; further workup including stool sample testing may be considered if symptoms persist
- Patient education provided regarding diagnosis and treatment plan; patient verbalized understanding with no further questions
