Fixed this session

QuickUMLS's 10x groundedness regression — root cause and fix

Rather than guess, this pulled up an unrestricted QuickUMLS matcher (package defaults, no threshold/semtype tightening) and tested it directly against clinically-relevant phrases the tuned matcher was dropping.

Gap 1 — the similarity threshold (0.85) was too strict for legitimate matches

"smoke"→"smoker" only reaches similarity 0.75. "peanuts"→"peanut" reaches 0.80. "allergic"→"allergican" reaches 0.75. All three sit below the 0.85 bar, despite their semantic types already being accepted.

The threshold raise (0.7→0.85) was originally meant to fix false positives like "start"/"well" being matched — but both of those were exact similarity=1.0 matches, immune to any threshold change. The denylist added alongside the threshold raise was what actually fixed those. The threshold raise itself was mostly just costing real recall. Lowered back to 0.75: still above the original too-permissive 0.7 default, but low enough to keep these legitimate matches.

Gap 2 — two semantic types were missing entirely

"lung cancer" (family history) only matches under T191 (Neoplastic Process). "loss of appetite" partly matches under T184 (Sign or Symptom). Neither was in the accepted set — T184 in particular is a clear oversight, given "Sign or Symptom" should be central to a clinical-relevance filter. Both added.

"travel" and "contacts" remain uncovered (under T058/T067/T170) — deliberately left out, since those are the same broad/abstract types that caused the original spurious-match problem. A known residual gap, not a fix worth the false-positive risk.
