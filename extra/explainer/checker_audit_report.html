Per checker: the theory vs. the run

What each one is trying to be good at, and the specific, real flag that shows why it isn't yet — every quote below is an actual false positive from that checker's own log.

EmbedKdeChecker

P 0.12 · R 0.50 · F1 0.20

Could shine

Cheapest possible signal — no training data, no API cost, catches content that's "distributionally unusual" against the reference text's own vocabulary.

Why not here

Paraphrase-blind: scispaCy's static word vectors barely connect "abdominal" and "tummy" (cosine 0.16) even though they're the same body part — a true claim phrased differently from the transcript's own words scores as unsupported. Extremely bandwidth-sensitive: an untuned bandwidth oversmoothed everything into looking typical; a properly cross-validated one overcorrected into treating any non-exact vocabulary match as wildly anomalous.

Only tested on 3 files so far — its 0.50 recall isn't proven at the scale the other six were run at.

AlignScoreChecker

P 0.06 · R 0.06 · F1 0.06

Could shine

A real trained entailment model, not word-density. Hand-tested this session against 6 short candidate phrases and got a clean, fully monotonic ranking — true content scored 0.30–0.86, false content 0.08–0.21.

Why not here

Real SOAP sentences aren't short clean phrases. Splitting on literal full stops only (as configured for this run) lets newline-separated, period-sparse SOAP fragments concatenate into one oversized multi-fact claim, which then fails entailment as a whole even when every individual fact in it is true.

Flagged as one hallucination: "PMH: Asthma / DH: Inhalers / SH: works as an accountant." — three separate, individually plausible facts glued into one claim by the lack of a period between them.

MedspacyUmlsChecker

P 0.01 · R 0.24 · F1 0.03

Could shine

The only checker here that separates omission / hallucination / status flip as three distinct judgments — and it's the only one that caught real negation reversals this session, e.g. "denies suicidal ideation" correctly matched to its flipped SOAP version.

Why not here

Its UMLS semantic-type net is broad (body parts, generic clinical attributes, procedures), so most flags are non-clinical noise. ConText's negation scope also bleeds across clause boundaries within one merged sentence or turn, producing false status flips on top of the volume problem.

Flagged as an omission: "stools: affirmed" — sourced from a doctor's question about stool consistency, not a real assertion.

MetaMapCuiChecker

P 0.01 · R 0.29 · F1 0.02

Could shine

The simplest possible baseline: normalize both documents to UMLS CUIs, diff the sets, no assertion modeling at all. Built specifically to isolate how much signal comes from concept-matching alone. It's the best answer to that question this dataset has: 0.625 recall on hallucinations, the highest of any deterministic checker here.

Why not here

That same 0.625 recall proves the underlying concept matching has real signal — but precision collapses under the same generic-concept flood as MedspacyUmlsChecker, with no negation layer at all to even partially filter it.

Flagged "stomach" as an omission three separate times from the same single transcript line.

DeterministicChecker

P 0.00 · R 0.00 · F1 0.00

Could shine

The most complete design on paper: concept matching + negation + numeric-attribute extraction (dosage/BP/temperature/duration/frequency) + drug-frame binding, layered specifically to catch "right drug, wrong number" errors nothing else here even attempts.

Why not here

Zero true positives across all 10 files, no exceptions. Two compounding causes: it inherits the same concept-noise problem as the other UMLS-based checkers, and its numeric-attribute flags are phrased as bare facts ("dosage 400mg") that structurally can't text-match this dataset's sentence-level ground-truth labels even on the rare occasion the underlying finding is correct — a label-granularity mismatch as much as a detection failure.

Flagged as a hallucination: "contacts (negated)" — a bare concept fragment with no sentence context to match against.

CqlChecker

P 0.00 · R 0.00 · F1 0.00

Could shine

A genuinely different kind of check — not "is this concept present" but "do the concepts that ARE present satisfy a cross-resource clinical rule," e.g. an antibiotic ordered with no documented condition to justify it. Catches a class of error invisible to any pure concept diff.

Why not here

Zero flags across all 10 files — not one false positive, not one true positive. This isn't imprecision, it's that its 2 hand-written rules never fired at all: this dataset's injected errors (entity swaps, inserted sentences, flipped negations, edited numbers) don't happen to trip either rule's specific pattern. The rule taxonomy and this dataset's error taxonomy simply don't overlap yet.

Needs many more rules, covering more resource-relationship patterns, before it can be judged on this dataset at all.

Pipeline_Merged (ensemble)

P 0.02 · R 0.24 · F1 0.03

Could shine

Combine several independent weak signals, keep only what multiple checkers agree on — the standard fix when individual detectors are noisy in different, uncorrelated ways.

Why not here

The checkers being merged aren't making independent errors — they mostly share the same root cause (an overly broad UMLS concept net). Merging noise that's correlated just carries it forward instead of cancelling it out. F1 beat 5 of the 6 individual checkers, but that's a low bar, not a fix.

LLM-as-judge (AI_checker / test4.py)

not logged in Evaluate

Could shine

Genuine contextual, compositional reasoning over the whole claim — not bag-of-concepts or bag-of-words matching, which is the structural reason it isn't affected by the generic-concept-flood problem every checker above shares.

Why it's different here

Never run through this project's formal Evaluate harness, so there's no logged precision/recall to put in the scoreboard above. But in live spot-checks this session (test4.py, mirroring a published inference-aware judge framework), it got 4 for 4 correct tier classifications — including the exact paraphrase case ("abdominal pain" as a paraphrase of "pain in my lower abdomen") that broke every embedding-based checker above.

Promising, not proven: 4 hand-picked spot-checks on one file is a much smaller sample than the 10-file runs everything else above was scored on.

On the proposed narrow-scope pipeline

Condense → extract only medications/diagnoses via medspaCy+UMLS → check presence + negation against the transcript → numeric checks → LASA checks. The diagnosis behind it is well-supported by the scoreboard above: MetaMapCuiChecker's bare CUI diff already gets 0.625 recall on hallucinations — the signal is real, it's just diluted by checking hundreds of low-value concepts alongside the few that matter. Narrowing scope is the right lever.

medications → allergies → red-flag diagnoses → everything else

Suggested risk ranking: medications carry the shortest, most mechanistic harm path (wrong substance + wrong dose = direct physiological harm) and are where LASA and numeric errors both live. Allergies are worth their own top tier, not folded into "issues" — a missed/hallucinated allergy is one of the most well-documented causes of preventable harm. Diagnoses are uneven risk: a missed sepsis/MI/anaphylaxis is not the same as a missed viral URTI.

1. Condense the transcript

Already validated in this project (MedspacyCondenser).

Risk: if the condenser drops a turn that's later the real evidence for a claim, the checker can't see it — it reports a false hallucination for something the patient genuinely said, just filtered out upstream. Check against the original transcript; use the condensed version only for anything human-facing.

2. Extract only medications + diagnoses (+ allergies) via medspaCy/UMLS

Narrows the semantic-type net from "everything clinically adjacent" to the categories that actually carry patient-safety risk — directly targets the dilution problem the scoreboard shows.

3. Check presence + negation against the transcript

Same ConText assertion layer as MedspacyUmlsChecker, just over a much smaller concept set.

Risk: narrowing scope reduces how often ConText's same-line scope-bleed fires, but doesn't eliminate it — a negation trigger can still bleed across an unrelated clause onto a real medication or diagnosis mention, not just onto generic words.

4. Numeric checks (dosage, frequency, duration)

Catches "right drug, wrong number" — the exact gap DeterministicChecker's design already names.

Risk: presence-checking alone ("is this number anywhere in the other doc") isn't enough — a number found somewhere isn't necessarily attached to the right drug. Use frame-binding (a window around each drug mention) the way DeterministicChecker's own design already specifies, not bare presence.

5. LASA checks

A real, well-documented medication-error class (hydralazine/hydroxyzine, Celebrex/Celexa). A static ISMP confused-name list + fuzzy string matching is bounded and deterministic; the existing LLM-based version (AI_checker.py) is more flexible but nondeterministic and rate-limited — worth keeping both, static list first, LLM as a fallback for coverage.
