Lower isn’t automatically better here — Medspacy’s bar goes up because its untuned version was over-filtering, dropping real clinical content along with the fluff. What matters is precision, not just how much gets cut. See each module below for what specifically changed.

Medspacy

Keeps a turn if it contains a hand-authored clinical target term

What was broken

The target-rule list had only ~24 hand-typed terms — symptoms and conditions, but nothing for social history, diet/exposure, or the treatment plan. Any turn without one of those exact 24 words was discarded, including genuinely relevant content.

What changed

Expanded the list to ~60 terms across social history (smoking, alcohol), exposure history (takeaway, restaurant, travel), and plan/management language (review, recommend, analgesia, sample) — using regex patterns instead of exact strings so word families match without needing a full lemmatizer pipeline.

Dropped beforep: Um, yeah, I ate at a Chinese restaurant with friends.
Dropped befored: Um, there are things like Dioralyte you can get from the pharmacy…
Kept afterp: Um, yeah, I ate at a Chinese restaurant with friends.
Kept afterd: Um, there are things like Dioralyte you can get from the pharmacy…

SciSpacy

Keeps a turn if scispacy’s biomedical NER finds any entity at all

What was broken

en_core_sci_sm exposes exactly one flat entity label — ENTITY — with no semantic type to filter on. Trained on formal scientific literature, it was tagging ordinary discourse markers as entities in spoken dialogue.

What changed

Added a shared denylist of confirmed false-positive spans and required at least one non-denylisted entity before a turn counts as clinical.

False positived: “Hello” · “Yeah” · “okay” · “Good morning” · “morning” — all tagged ENTITY
False positived: “Great” · “I wish” — both tagged ENTITY, from “Great. Well, I wish you all the best.”

Negspacy

Same scispacy NER, plus negation detection — keeps only un-negated entities

What was broken

Identical root cause to SciSpacy: it inherits en_core_sci_sm’s single flat label, so the negation layer was correctly checking whether a discourse marker was negated — a well-formed answer to the wrong question.

What changed

Same denylist, applied before the negation check: a turn is kept only if it has an un-negated entity that also isn’t a known false positive.

QuickUMLS

Fuzzy string matching against a 6.1M-term local UMLS index

What was broken

Two independent issues stacked here. First, the defaults (similarity threshold 0.7, 27 accepted semantic types) were too permissive for casual dialogue. Second — and this one no threshold can fix — UMLS contains ordinary English words as literal clinical terminology: “start” is a real T061 (Therapeutic/Preventive Procedure) concept, and “well” is a real T033 (Finding) concept, both matched at exact similarity 1.0.

What changed

Raised the threshold to 0.85 and narrowed accepted types from 27 down to 15 focused on symptoms, findings, procedures and drugs. Then, since exact matches ignore any threshold, added a denylist for the specific generic words confirmed by direct inspection of the match output.

Kept befored: Hello? Hi. Um, should we start? … Good morning sir, how can I help you this morning?
Kept befored: Thank you. Bye bye.
Dropped after— both turns above no longer pass the clinical-relevance filter —

Shared root cause

One bug was hiding inside all four

Every transcript carries its own transcription-uncertainty markup — <UNSURE>…</UNSURE> and <UNIN/> — and none of the four condensers were stripping it before analysis. The literal word “UNSURE” coincidentally matches a real UMLS concept (T033, Finding) at exact similarity 1.0, which is exactly why greeting and sign-off turns kept surviving QuickUMLS no matter how the thresholds were tuned.

Confirmed present in all 57 transcript files and absent from all SOAP notes — a transcript-only artifact. A shared clean_transcript() step now strips it once, upstream of every module, in both main.py and NLP_Main.py.

All figures above come from direct runs of the actual condenser code on prim57/cleaned transcripts/prim1.txt, not estimates. SciSpacy and Negspacy’s “before” figures are the 57-file, 5-run dataset average (3.5% and 6.5% reduction respectively) from the last full NLP_Main.py pass, since that pass predates today’s fixes; Medspacy and QuickUMLS’s “before” figures are this same file, untuned.
